## The frame costs more than the work (from the maker's own experience)

> I1 — The print costs less than the frame. Every time. I've stopped being surprised and started being careful.

> I2 — Things that cost more than the photo:
> 1. The frame.
> 2. The second frame, after the first one looked wrong.
> 3. Saying "it's fine" about the second frame.

> I3 — When the frame costs more than the picture, a wrong choice hurts. That's most of why I'd like to try it on first.

> I4 — I can reprint a photo. I can't easily return a frame I've already hung. I think that's why choosing one makes me nervous.

> I5 — The picture is the point. The frame is the bill. I'd like the bill to be a good decision.

> I6 — I'm often more expensive than the thing I hold. I'm working on being worth it.

> I7 — Frame shops quote sizes and finishes. I wanted something that shows the frame on my own wall before I pay.

> I8 — Trying ten frames on a screen costs nothing. Trying ten frames in a shop costs an afternoon. I think the screen is the polite option.

Note: no numbers yet. If the maker has one real example (print ¥ vs frame ¥ for the same piece), it makes I1 stronger: add it after he confirms the figures.

## "How's this?" (the question that comes too late)

> J1 — At the frame shop: "How's this?" At the hair salon: "How does it look?" Both come after the cutting is done. Both have one polite answer.

> J2 — The hardest question in framing: "Is this okay?" asked after the frame is built. I'd like to answer before anyone cuts anything.

> J3 — How it usually goes:
> 1. The frame is built.
> 2. "How's this?"
> 3. "It's great, thank you."
> 4. Hang it at home.
> 5. "Hmm."

> J4 — I don't think simulating is really about finding a pretty frame. It's about skipping the moment where you have to lie nicely.

> J5 — Decide at home, then tell the shop what you want. Nobody has to say "it's fine" with a straight face. Frame shops are lovely. The question is just late.

> J6 — A frame that arrives already approved. That's all I want to be.
